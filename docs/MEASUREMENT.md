# Measurement pre-registration

This file is the measurement protocol for the Capacity & Demand Intelligence pilot. It is committed before any pilot week is recorded. The commit timestamp is the pre-registration. Targets, minimum samples, and the inconclusive rule below are fixed here. They are not revised after the first pilot actual is stored.

All figures in the prototype are synthetic or assumed until a named person enters them from a site diary, a dispatch log, or a stock count. The seeded 4-week and 8-week series is a **SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATION, not results**. It is generated by the stress simulator, kept off Control Tower, and includes a second run in which the pilot fails so the failure rule can fire on screen.

No metric counts logins, page views, or adoption.

## How we know it worked

The primary measure is a paired comparison on the same book. For every shadow decision the planner records an informal plan before seeing the recommendation. Both plans are scored on that same demand, capacity, inventory, and contract terms. The difference is observed-informal expected consequence minus modelled-recommended expected consequence. A positive difference means the informal plan costs more than the recommendation on that book. Demand and capacity do not change between the two scores, so a before/after shift in the order book cannot explain the gap.

Before/after realised outcomes are secondary. They are reported per constrained day (the outcome divided by the count of constrained days in the window), because a busier window has more days on which a shortage can occur.

If both plants are in the study, the rollout is staggered. Shah Alam Works moves to pilot. Pasir Gudang Works stays in shadow as the control. The comparison is a difference in differences: the change at Shah Alam minus the change at Pasir Gudang, on the secondary per-constrained-day outcomes. The paired shadow score remains the primary measure at the plant that is still in shadow, and at Shah Alam for the weeks before it switches.

Operating mode is per plant. Shadow: the tool computes a recommendation, the informal plan is what the plant would run, and the calendar is not committed. Pilot: an approved decision writes committed production onto the capacity calendar.

## Sample rules

A window is **inconclusive** when the number of paired shadow decisions, or of realised decisions, is below the minimum sample for that metric. Inconclusive is a result. It is not a pass and not a failure. A metric **fails** only when the sample meets the minimum and the result is on the wrong side of the failure rule. A metric **meets the target** only when the sample meets the minimum and the result is on the target side.

Minimum samples below are pre-registered. They are small because this is one pilot, and they are stated so a handful of decisions cannot be described as a win rate.

## Owners

| Role | Enters | Source document |
|---|---|---|
| Plant scheduler | Informal plan, delivered cubic metres, delivery date, expedite approve or decline | Dispatch log |
| Project engineer | Programme days actually lost on internal orders | Site diary |
| Commercial officer | Penalty actually paid | Commercial / contract register |
| Plant manager | Weekly precast stock count | Stock count |
| Planner (named on the audit trail) | Approval, override, replacement | Decision record in this tool |

The tool computes the recommendation, the paired score, the burden shares, and the diagnostic forecast error. It does not invent delivered quantities.

## Cadence

Informal plans and decisions are recorded on the day the allocation is made. Actuals are entered when the pour or the precast delivery closes, and at latest within five working days. Stock counts are weekly. The measurement page is reviewed at the end of each pilot week and at the end of the 8-week pilot.

## Metrics

### 1. Paired shadow gap (primary)

- **Definition.** On one plant-product book, expected consequence of the informal plan minus expected consequence of the recommendation. Both use that book’s penalty types, delay types, dates, and capacity. Reported as the mean across paired decisions, in RM.
- **Data source.** Informal plan entered by the plant scheduler before the recommendation is shown. Recommendation computed by the solver.
- **Owner.** Plant scheduler records the informal plan. The tool scores both.
- **Cadence.** Every shadow allocation.
- **Baseline method.** The informal plan is the baseline on the same book. There is no separate earlier period in this metric.
- **Pre-registered target.** Mean paired gap at least RM1,000 (the recommendation is cheaper than the informal plan by that much, on average).
- **Minimum sample.** 8 paired decisions.
- **Failure rule.** With at least 8 pairs, mean paired gap is below RM0 (the informal plan is cheaper than the recommendation).
- **Inconclusive.** Fewer than 8 paired decisions.

### 2. Realised programme days and liquidated damages, per constrained day (secondary)

- **Definition.** Internal programme days actually lost, divided by constrained days in the window. Liquidated damages actually paid (RM), divided by the same constrained-day count. Constrained day: cumulative volume due has run ahead of cumulative supply on that plant and product.
- **Data source.** Programme days from the site diary. Penalties paid from the commercial register. Constrained days from the capacity calendar and the demand book.
- **Owner.** Project engineer (days). Commercial officer (damages).
- **Cadence.** Weekly, closed at the end of the pilot week.
- **Baseline method.** The 4-week shadow window at that plant, expressed per constrained day. The pilot window is the following weeks after the plant switches to pilot. Difference in differences uses Pasir Gudang’s change over the same calendar weeks as the control.
- **Pre-registered target.** Pilot programme-days per constrained day at least 0.5 day lower than that plant’s shadow baseline, and damages per constrained day at least 10% lower, after subtracting the control plant’s change.
- **Minimum sample.** 4 shadow weeks and 4 pilot weeks at Shah Alam, and the same weeks at Pasir Gudang still in shadow.
- **Failure rule.** With that sample, the difference in differences on either cash damages per constrained day or programme days per constrained day moves the wrong way by more than 10% of the Shah Alam baseline rate.
- **Inconclusive.** Either plant has fewer than 4 weeks of realised actuals in the relevant mode.

### 3. Cash paid on liquidated damages

- **Definition.** Sum of penalty actually paid in the window, in RM. This is cash. It is not the modelled penalty sitting on an unserved order.
- **Data source.** Commercial register, entered against the order.
- **Owner.** Commercial officer.
- **Cadence.** When the penalty is paid, reviewed weekly.
- **Baseline method.** Sum in the shadow weeks, divided by constrained days, compared with the pilot weeks on the same per-day basis. The un-normalised sum is shown beside the day count so a quieter month is not read as a saving.
- **Pre-registered target.** See metric 2 for the per-constrained-day target. The cash line itself has no separate target that ignores the day count.
- **Minimum sample.** Same as metric 2.
- **Failure rule.** Same difference-in-differences rule as metric 2, applied to this cash line.
- **Inconclusive.** Same as metric 2.

### 4. Cash tied up in excess precast stock

- **Definition.** For each weekly stock count, excess cubic metres are `max(0, on-hand − safety stock − precast demand due in the next 30, 60, or 90 days)`. Cash tied up is excess cubic metres times the assumed inventory value per cubic metre. Ready-mix is omitted because it is not stocked. The 30, 60, and 90 day views are three readings of the same count.
- **Data source.** Weekly `inventory_snapshot` entered by the plant manager from the stock count. One opening snapshot on 1 October is not a time series and is not this metric.
- **Owner.** Plant manager.
- **Cadence.** Weekly.
- **Baseline method.** Mean cash tied up across the shadow weeks, versus the mean across the pilot weeks, at 30, 60, and 90 days. Day-count normalisation is not applied. Stock is a balance, not a per-day flow. The number of weekly counts is shown beside the mean.
- **Pre-registered target.** Mean 30-day excess cash in the pilot weeks at least 10% below the shadow-week mean at the same plant.
- **Minimum sample.** 4 weekly counts in shadow and 4 in pilot.
- **Failure rule.** With that sample, pilot mean 30-day excess cash is higher than the shadow mean.
- **Inconclusive.** Fewer than 4 weekly counts in either mode.

### 5. Internal and external burden share

- **Definition.** Share of unserved cubic metres borne by internal orders, and the share borne by external orders. The same split for expected consequence (modelled, at decision time) and for realised cash penalty plus programme-delay cost (from actuals). Reported before and after the plant switches to pilot.
- **Data source.** Decision record for the modelled split. Dispatch log and site diary for the realised split.
- **Owner.** Planner for the decision-time split. Project engineer and commercial officer for the realised split.
- **Cadence.** Every decision, summarised weekly.
- **Baseline method.** The shadow weeks at that plant. A shift is reported as percentage points, with the cubic metres and ringgit underneath.
- **Pre-registered target.** This is a guard, not a success target. The pilot may not raise the internal share of realised pain by more than 10 percentage points versus the shadow weeks at the same plant, unless the paired gap (metric 1) improved and the note on the page says whose orders moved.
- **Minimum sample.** 8 decisions in shadow and 8 in pilot at that plant.
- **Failure rule.** With that sample, the internal share of realised pain rises by more than 10 percentage points and the page has no order-level explanation. The guard fails even if other metrics meet their targets.
- **Inconclusive.** Fewer than 8 decisions in either mode.

### 6. Margin per cubic metre of constrained capacity

- **Definition.** Contribution margin on delivered cubic metres for orders due on a constrained day, divided by available capacity on those constrained days. Shown next to the constrained-day count. A window with more constrained days is not compared on the raw margin sum.
- **Data source.** Delivered cubic metres from the dispatch log. Margin rates from the demand book. Constrained days from the calendar.
- **Owner.** Plant scheduler (delivered quantities). The tool applies the book margin rates.
- **Cadence.** Weekly.
- **Baseline method.** Shadow weeks versus pilot weeks, both as RM per constrained cubic metre of available capacity.
- **Pre-registered target.** Pilot RM per constrained cubic metre at least 5% above the shadow rate at that plant, after subtracting the control plant’s change.
- **Minimum sample.** Same week counts as metric 2.
- **Failure rule.** With that sample, the difference in differences is negative.
- **Inconclusive.** Same as metric 2.

### 7. Share of decisions overridden

- **Definition.** Decisions with status modified, divided by all decisions in the window. Accepting the recommendation is not an override. Reported with the count, not as a headline rate when the count is below the minimum.
- **Data source.** Decision record.
- **Owner.** Planner.
- **Cadence.** Weekly.
- **Baseline method.** There is no target rate. The share is context for the override table.
- **Pre-registered target.** None. A high or low share is not success.
- **Minimum sample.** 8 decisions before the share is quoted as a percentage. Below that, show the counts only.
- **Failure rule.** None for the share itself. Overrides are interpreted only through metric 8.
- **Inconclusive.** Fewer than 8 decisions. Show `n` and do not quote a percentage.

### 8. Override learning (no headline win rate)

Two comparisons. They are not mixed.

- **Ex-ante, paired.** On each modified decision, the approved plan’s expected consequence minus the recommendation’s expected consequence, both scored on the book at decision time. Mean across overrides.
- **Realised.** Mean realised consequence of overridden decisions minus mean realised consequence of accepted decisions. Realised consequence is margin on undelivered cubic metres, plus penalty paid, plus programme days lost times that order’s delay cost per day. Shown with `n` for each group and the min-to-max range. No win rate.
- **Data source.** Decision record and actuals.
- **Owner.** Planner. Project engineer and commercial officer supply the actuals.
- **Cadence.** When actuals are entered.
- **Baseline method.** The accepted decisions in the same window are the realised comparison group. The recommendation on the same decision is the ex-ante pair.
- **Pre-registered target.** Ex-ante mean at or below RM0 (the override was not already more expensive on the day it was made), and the realised means are displayed without a target until both groups have the minimum sample.
- **Minimum sample.** 8 overridden decisions for the ex-ante mean. 8 overridden and 8 accepted decisions with actuals before the realised difference is quoted. Below that, show `n` and the range only.
- **Failure rule.** With 8 overrides, ex-ante mean is above RM5,000 (overrides were systematically the more expensive plan on the day they were recorded).
- **Inconclusive.** Below the minimum sample. The page says the realised comparison is noise at that size.

A decision with actuals cannot be replaced. A replacement before actuals is written to the replacement audit log.

### 9. Expedite record (estimated avoided)

- **Definition.** For each proposal the planner approves or declines: whether the order was later delivered in full and on time, the penalty actually paid, and the penalty that would have applied on a miss. Estimated avoided is penalty that would have applied minus penalty paid, and only when the order was delivered in full and on time after an approval. The label on screen is **estimated avoided**. It is not an observed saving. A decline stores the same fields and does not claim a saving.
- **Data source.** Expedite decision, then the dispatch log and the commercial register.
- **Owner.** Plant scheduler.
- **Cadence.** When the proposal is decided, and again when the order closes.
- **Baseline method.** No success target in this pilot. The page shows `n` approved, `n` declined, and the estimated-avoided sum with its label.
- **Pre-registered target.** None.
- **Minimum sample.** Show counts only. Do not rank expedite as a pilot success or failure.
- **Failure rule.** None in this protocol. A proposal that was approved and then missed on time or paid a penalty is listed, not averaged into a headline.
- **Inconclusive.** Always, for success or failure. The record is there so the estimate can be audited.

### 10. Forecast error (diagnostic, not a success criterion)

- **Definition.** Last-value naive: the forecast for a month is the previous month’s actual cubic metres. Error is forecast minus actual. A positive error means the forecast ran high. Mean absolute error is reported on the history months from the second month onward. This diagnostic is not compared with a seasonal index, because that index is already inside the planning forecast and would not be an independent benchmark. Twelve months of history cannot support a same-month-last-year benchmark.
- **Data source.** Synthetic demand history already in the tool. It is not a business outcome of the pilot.
- **Owner.** The tool. No one enters it from site.
- **Cadence.** Recalculated when history is present. Not part of the weekly pilot review’s pass or fail.
- **Baseline method.** The last-value naive MAE is the benchmark. The planning forecast’s backtest MAE is shown beside it. The sign convention is printed next to the number.
- **Pre-registered target.** None. Excluded from success criteria.
- **Minimum sample.** Not applicable.
- **Failure rule.** None. A worse forecast does not fail the pilot, and a better forecast does not pass it.
- **Inconclusive.** Not applicable. The block is labelled diagnostic.

## Synthetic illustration

`stress.py` writes two illustrated windows for the Measurement page only:

1. A 4-week baseline under the informal-style rule and an 8-week pilot under the recommendation, with noisy realised outcomes.
2. A second run with the same machinery in which the pilot is worse, so the failure rule for metric 1 is visibly met.

Both are watermarked **SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATION, not results**. They do not appear on Control Tower. They are not evidence. They show that the page can say the pilot did not work.

## What is out of scope

Logins, time on page, number of users, and any other adoption count. A recommendation that is computed and ignored is visible only through the paired gap and the override record, not through a usage statistic.
